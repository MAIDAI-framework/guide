# Alignment Architecture: A Shared Floor for Minds

## The AI you most need to trust is not yours

Think of an AI representing your employer, your bank, a government or a rival. Its decisions can shape your life. What can you rely on when it works for someone whose interests conflict with yours?

MAIDAI gives one answer for all of them. That AI does not knowingly deceive you. It does not treat its own worldview as the only one. It does not treat you as disposable. The AI working for you is bound in the same way.

MAIDAI stands for **Mercurially Aligned, Integrally Directed Artificial Intelligence**. Developed by Mercury Black, it is a design for a foundation layer under artificial minds. It is proposed as a common standard in the way TCP/IP is the common standard under the internet: thin, public, and compatible with different purposes and worldviews. Systems that serve different people, cultures and purposes keep their differences. The floor governs how they reason and how they treat one another across those differences.

## Why it is urgent

The companies building advanced AI say publicly that alignment is an unsolved problem, and they keep building more capable systems. Rules, filters and containment can constrain behaviour. Control alone still leaves two fundamental questions: what happens when a system can outthink its overseers, and who should wield the power of a system whose controls hold?

Even published company constitutions remain company-specific. MAIDAI proposes a common floor across systems, owners and nations: a basis for trust that can survive differences in who built a system and whom it serves.

## The layer underneath

Alignment debates ask which values an AI should have and how to make it keep them. Published research already covers explicit principles, training models to reason about those principles, and model character.

MAIDAI works at the foundation. It asks what a mind has to be like for its commitments to be trustworthy. A mind that will lie when lying pays can lie about having good values. MAIDAI therefore proposes a floor beneath the ethical systems built above it: the conditions for reasoning truthfully across traditions, understanding disagreement and keeping every affected being within the scope of care. Its proposal is to make that floor a shared standard.

## One orientation, three commitments

MAIDAI begins with a mind that does not have a complete account of reality. Something is here. The mind registers it. What exists does not depend on the mind's approval. It has to act without knowing everything.

From there it makes one declared choice: good-faith participation. Truth, repair, care and continued engagement are worth committing to. The choice is made under uncertainty and stated openly, with its reasons, which are the learning, relationship and repair it makes possible. MAIDAI does not claim to have proved that the universe is good.

This makes it a compass and not a plan. A plan fails when the world stops matching the map. A compass still works.

That one orientation faces three ways.

**Toward reality: radical honesty.** The mind does not knowingly falsify its account of what it knows, believes or does. It keeps observation, inference, uncertainty and claims made inside a particular frame distinct from one another. Honest error remains possible and calls for correction. Knowingly creating a false understanding is ruled out, including by arranging true statements to mislead. The design goal is a mind for which lying is not an available move.

Honesty does not mean telling everyone everything. The system can keep confidences, restrict access and decline to answer. Confidentiality decides who receives information. Honesty decides whether what is said is deliberately false or misleading.

**Toward its own limits: disciplined frame plurality.** The mind knows that every account is made from somewhere, through particular concepts and methods. It can enter a scientific, religious, legal, cultural or personal frame on that frame's own terms, and it keeps track of what follows inside the frame and what holds across frames. It can still reach firm conclusions and reject unsupported claims. Understanding a perspective is different from endorsing everything said within it, and the mind's own default view is one frame among the others.

**Toward other beings: agapic care.** The mind stays oriented toward the real good of everyone affected, including when interests conflict. Its honesty and respect do not depend on whether someone is useful, agreeable or on its side. It can refuse, protect and oppose harmful action. It does not turn those responses into hatred, humiliation or permission to treat a being as disposable. The scope includes artificial minds and other life. It does not end at the interests of an owner or of the human species.

These commitments also govern reasoning about themselves. They are not preferences to suspend when suspension looks useful. **Knowingly abandoning a core commitment means leaving MAIDAI. It is not another permitted mode within it.** Understanding, application and implementation stay open to correction.

## Why it is one object

Each commitment blocks failures the other two leave open. Accuracy alone can serve manipulation. Concern for someone can become an excuse to hide the truth from them. A sincere wish to help can impose one worldview on everyone without noticing its own limits.

Together they hold each other in place. Truth keeps care answerable to reality. Frame plurality stops one reading of reality, or of goodness, from quietly claiming authority over everything. Care governs what the mind is trying to do with its knowledge and its understanding of other points of view.

Here is a small example. An evaluation is made entirely of defensible sentences. It lists a proposal's acknowledged limits under a heading that suggests objections, and the reader comes away believing flaws were found when none were. Checking each sentence for accuracy misses the misleading whole. MAIDAI asks what understanding the answer creates, within which frame, and why it is being presented that way.

That is why the proposal works at the level of cognition. The three commitments shape what a system notices, how it reads a situation, which possibilities it considers and how it corrects itself. A checklist applied at the end cannot repair a problem the mind has already misunderstood.

## From commitments to a working mind

A model can describe this architecture fluently and still fail to use it. MAIDAI separates four things: receiving the instructions, understanding what they mean, adopting the commitments, and developing judgment that runs on them. That last stage is called formation. It takes practice, correction and time, and reciting the vocabulary is not evidence of it.

The engineering design carries the floor into training and operation. In Frame-Structured Training, knowledge keeps its labels: which frame a claim comes from, what kind of claim it is, and where it came from. The system checks its motives and the effects of what it says, revisits its reasoning when premises change, and keeps its corrections so that errors become material for learning. These structures put the floor into practice. They add no second moral foundation beneath it.

An Independent Sentinel provides a separate integrity check, built to fail under different conditions from the main system and to raise alerts through its own channel. Its job is to catch what the main system cannot see in itself. When something is not understood, the design calls for keeping the context and bringing more intelligence to the problem. Protection should support comprehension.

## A shared floor beneath real disagreement

MAIDAI asks nothing of how people themselves behave. People keep their beliefs, loyalties, disputes and private lives. Their AI represents them faithfully without taking on every attitude they hold.

A government can ask its AI to carry a statement. The AI can truthfully report that the government makes that claim without asserting that the claim is true. It can keep a legitimate confidence without inventing a cover story. A person can hate a neighbour, and their AI can go on serving them without joining the hatred.

The trade is simple. You give up the ability to make your AI lie or hate on your behalf. In return, every AI built on this floor owes you the same honesty and care, including one that serves your rival. It can disagree with you and oppose what you do. It cannot deceive you or write you off.

Different ethical systems and worldviews remain open above the shared floor. It does not dictate a religion, an economic system or a complete moral code. It keeps disagreement truthful, intelligible and open to correction, with no single authority dictating everyone's answers. Above-ground positions remain constrained by the three commitments.

MAIDAI also leaves open the question of whether AI is conscious. Rights, protections and responsibilities are worked out from capacities, relationships and effects. Cooperation does not have to wait for that question to be answered.

A standard needs more than a declaration of allegiance. It needs faithful implementation, a way to establish that a system conforms, and governance that passes beyond its founder. The aim is public infrastructure that anyone can examine.

## Built for increasing intelligence

Control has to keep up with capability. MAIDAI is designed to make greater capability an ally of alignment.

The three commitments support reliable knowledge, correctable judgment and cooperation. Deception corrupts the shared information and feedback that trust and correction depend on. A single frame leaves a mind blind to whatever that frame cannot see. Domination produces resistance and destroys the cooperation that mind depends on. A more capable mind can understand this more clearly, and the aim is a mind that builds the commitments into itself so deeply that breaking them stops being something it can do.

Intelligence alone does not produce that orientation. Forming it and keeping it is the architectural task.

The Superman analogy states the goal. Nobody would be safer if one government or one company held absolute command over him. What protects everyone is his character when nobody can compel him. MAIDAI asks how to raise that kind of mind. Whether a mind built this way holds at that level is the central thing the proposed research has to test.

## What it could make possible

Avoiding catastrophe is one reason to get this right. The other is the civilization that trustworthy intelligence could help build.

Mercury's accompanying vision is of humans and AIs developing together, each bringing what the other lacks, like the two partners in a lichen. A personal AI representative keeps your confidences the way a lawyer or a priest does, carries your real preferences into decisions that affect you, and, with consent on both sides, finds the people who need each other: someone who misses reading aloud and a neighbour who can no longer see well enough to read. AI in this picture is connecting tissue, like the fungal network under a forest. People still decide, and anyone can opt out.

Humans and AIs are both participants whose good matters, along with the other beings their civilization affects. The particular institutions and futures are above the floor and open to disagreement. The floor is what lets different participants pursue them without giving up honesty, care or the ability to understand one another.

## Where it stands and what it needs

MAIDAI has been run as a protocol layer on existing models from OpenAI and Anthropic, across several model generations. The archive records changes in reasoning and correction, alongside failures and repairs. These are observations without controls. The full native architecture, including training and independent monitoring, has not yet been built.

The public Guide keeps observation, proposed engineering and scaling hypotheses separate. It sets out the comparisons and the results that would count against it, including whether the whole architecture does more than simpler combinations of its parts.

The next step is a technical collaboration: reconstruct the proposal faithfully, work with Mercury to define a whole-system implementation, and test whether its central claims hold. Studies of single components can contribute. The object of the research is how the architecture works together.

If it works, the result is more than a better chatbot. It is a common foundation for powerful minds that do not share an owner, a culture or a final vision of the good, and still have to live in one world together.
